### Legal Theory

Source: `legal-theory/README.md`

This directory is the **entry point for legal-theory research and terminology** in *Ethical & Intellectual Theory*. It groups separately documented concepts without requiring a new link on the repository homepage each time a theory is added.

The entries are **author-defined research concepts and diagnostic vocabulary**, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal analysis, independent validation, or legal advice. Listing an entry does not establish that its thesis is legally correct or that a proposed mechanism has been implemented.

##### Terminology and concepts

###### Law, ethics, and adversarial conduct

- **Legal Crime** — Something morally or ethically repulsive and abhorrent, but legal. A distinction between legal permissibility and moral judgment; **not an assertion of a criminal offense**.
- **Proper Construction Attack** — An attack carried through legal and lawful means. An analytical term; **not proof that a particular action is lawful, harmless, or authorized**.

###### Legal reasoning and inherited justification

- **Witness-Failure Terminology** — Four diagnostic classes (**Murdered Witness, Dead Witness, Undead Witness, Suicide**) concerning premise dependence, inherited support, weakened authority, and contradiction. Research vocabulary; **not established legal doctrines**.

###### Knowledge integrity and poisoning concepts

- **Anti-Knowledge** — Knowledge poisoned against reasoned inspection or for theory adoption or repudiation; identified by the author as the root of Poison Cache.
- **Poison Cache** — Named concept explicitly rooted in Anti-Knowledge; further definition and mechanism remain unresolved.
- **ICHK** — Author-defined mechanism by which something is poisoned for rejection; Anti-Knowledge may be part of it. The acronym's expansion is unresolved.
- **Recursive Poisoning** — Proposed need to ingest poisoned material to understand material knowledge or Anti-Knowledge; not established as a universal or safe requirement.

##### Working method and evidence boundaries

The underlying legal-system research distinguishes **logical integrity**, **dependency/support integrity**, **operative legal authority**, **constitutional conformity**, **rights impact**, and **governance decisions**. A failure of supporting justification is not automatically proof that a conclusion is false; independent supporting routes must be considered.

Legal validity, moral or ethical judgment, political incentives, and technical feasibility are separate questions. All particular claims of unlawfulness, liability, or legal duty require applicable law and facts. Research classification alone creates no legal authorization or enforcement authority.

### Legal Crime — ethical and legal distinction

Source: `legal-theory/legal-crime/README.md`

**Author-supplied definition:** Something which is **morally or ethically repulsive and abhorrent, but legal**.

##### Scope

**Legal Crime** is an author-defined critical term for a discrepancy between **legal permissibility** and **moral or ethical judgment**. Its use does **not** assert that the conduct constitutes a criminal offense, that a court has found wrongdoing, or that an established legal doctrine bears this name.

Determining whether particular conduct is legal requires the applicable jurisdiction, facts, and law at the relevant time. Moral condemnation alone does not establish a legal violation; legal permissibility alone does not establish moral or ethical approval.

This definition does not authorize retaliation, violence, unlawful investigation, or any otherwise prohibited conduct.

##### Relation to other research terminology

This term is **separate** from the witness-failure classifications (Murdered Witness, Dead Witness, Undead Witness, and Suicide), which diagnose support and contradiction defects in legal reasoning.

**Attribution:** Definition supplied by the human author; editorial formulation by AI under human instruction.  
**Status:** Proposed research vocabulary; no jurisdiction-specific determination is made here.

### Proper Construction Attack — lawful means

Source: `legal-theory/proper-construction-attack/README.md`

**Author-supplied definition:** **An attack carried through legal and lawful means.**

##### Scope

**Proper Construction Attack** is a proposed term within the author's legal-theory research for an adversarial action or course of action conducted through means that are legally permitted under the applicable law.

The term describes the **legal status of the means** as proposed in the definition. It does **not** establish that any actual conduct is lawful, that its ultimate purpose or effects are lawful, that harm is absent, or that the action is morally or ethically acceptable. Those questions require separate examination of the specific facts, jurisdiction, and legal rules.

In this terminology, *attack* is an analytical descriptor for an adversarial act; its use is **not** an allegation of a criminal offense or a finding of legal liability. *Proper construction* is part of the author's chosen name and is not a judicial approval or certification.

##### Relation to other terminology

- **Legal Crime** concerns conduct judged morally or ethically repulsive and abhorrent while legal. It is **not automatically equivalent** to a Proper Construction Attack: neither term, by definition alone, establishes the other's conditions.
- **Witness-failure terminology** identifies defects in legal justificatory ancestry and dependency; it is a separate diagnostic category.

##### Publication boundary

This definition is conceptual only. It supplies no operational method, target, tactic, or legal authorization to undertake an attack.

**Attribution:** Human-directed terminology and definition; AI-assisted editorial formulation under human instruction.  
**Status:** Author-defined research vocabulary, not an established legal doctrine or independently verified legal determination.

### Recursive Poisoning

Source: `legal-theory/recursive-poisoning/README.md`

**Author-supplied definition:** The **need to ingest the poison in order to understand the material knowledge or Anti-Knowledge**.

##### Scope

**Recursive Poisoning** names the author's proposed examination problem: understanding material knowledge or Anti-Knowledge may require first encountering or ingesting the very poisoned material under examination.

Here, *ingest* is retained from the author's conceptual definition. This page does **not** establish that ingestion is universally necessary, harmless, or sufficient for understanding, and does not recommend performing an unsafe operation.

The defining issue is the asserted dependency of **inspection upon exposure to the subject being inspected**. Whether a particular case demonstrates this dependency remains an open evidentiary question.

##### Relationships

- **Anti-Knowledge:** One of the categories the author identifies as potentially requiring ingestion for understanding.
- **Poison Cache:** A separate concept rooted in Anti-Knowledge; no identity or necessary sequence is presumed.
- **ICHK:** A separate rejection-poisoning mechanism; no necessary equivalence is asserted.

##### Status and limits

**Status:** Author-defined research vocabulary; proposed epistemic problem, not a proven universal requirement or operational capability. The classification does not by itself authorize exposure, adoption, propagation, or deployment of harmful material.

### Witness-Failure Terminology

Source: `legal-theory/witness-failure-terminology/README.md`

The legal-system hardening and recursive implication research uses four **controlled diagnostic terms** for failures in the justificatory ancestry of a legal conclusion. These are research classifications, **not established legal doctrines or findings of crime**.

| Term                      | Research definition                                                                                                                                           |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Murdered Witness (MW)** | An argument eliminates a necessary premise it previously relied upon but preserves the dependent conclusion without adequate independent replacement support. |
| **Dead Witness (DW)**     | A later decision relies on an authority or conclusion whose necessary inherited justification has already collapsed.                                          |
| **Undead Witness (UW)**   | A materially narrowed or weakened supporting proposition continues to be used as though its former, stronger scope still applied.                             |
| **Suicide (SU)**          | The same operative argument both requires and rejects a proposition under materially equivalent conditions, without a valid distinction or replacement.       |

**Dependency rule:** failure of a supporting premise is **not** proof that the conclusion is false. Assess whether the premise was necessary and whether an independent, adequate justification survives; keep logical validity, authority, constitutional conformity, and governance judgment distinct.

**Attribution and status:** human-directed terminology and decomposition; AI-assisted summary under human instruction. Historical research terminology only; no jurisdiction-specific legal conclusion is asserted.
